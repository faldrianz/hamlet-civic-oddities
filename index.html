Chapter one

The invocation.

What happens when anyone can sign up to open a meeting with a prayer?

Ottumwa, Iowa · Oct 6, 2026

A former council member steps to the mic.

Matthew Dalbey signed up, as any resident can. He reads:

“Dear Dark Father… unbowed and unfettered by arcane doctrines… Luciferian impulse to eat of the tree of knowledge… It is done. Hail Satan.”

The text comes from The Satanic Temple, a non-theistic group that uses Satan as a literary symbol of dissent.

Then the room reacts

1A pastor calls out: “reverse this curse.”
2The mayor calls for silence.
3The council had opened the mic in January (Res 18-2026). The lone dissenter had warned this could happen.
4Dalbey is rebooked for November.

Pensacola, Florida · 2016

Same script.
Different town.

David Suhor sings it in a hooded robe. The room answers with the Lord’s Prayer. About a third walk out.

“Thank you for not interrupting me.”

Similar cases: San Marcos TX, Battle Ground WA, Kenai Peninsula AK. In Tulsa OK, a pagan Medusa invocation was mislabeled “satanic.”

The rule · Town of Greece v. Galloway, 2014

Open the floor to any volunteer, and you can’t screen by content or creed.

So a council has only three exits:

1Keep the mic open for everyone.
2Switch to a moment of silence.
3Invite official chaplains only.

Chapter two

The peacocks.

Birds cross city lines. Rules don’t.

South Florida’s peafowl are non-native, traced to escapees from Coconut Grove. They scream at 2–4 a.m., dent cars and damage roofs.
